# Notion second brain: where it stalls a few months in

A second brain in Notion builds fine. It stalls a few months later. The notes have grown into the hundreds, and **you can no longer see what is tied to what**, so you fail to find things you know you wrote and write them again. It is the most common complaint from people who run a second brain in Notion, and it is not a filing problem.

## What a second brain is

The phrase comes from Tiago Forte's book *Building a Second Brain*. The idea is to keep what you read, think and work on in one trusted place outside your head, so that it can be found and reused later. His method has two parts that are often mixed up.

- **CODE** is the workflow: Capture, Organize, Distill, Express
- **PARA** is the filing system: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives

Notion suits both. Databases hold the notes, properties tag them, and templates make capture fast.

## The usual Notion setup

Most Notion second brains look alike.

- A **Projects** database for things with an end date
- An **Areas** database for ongoing responsibilities
- A **Resources** or Notes database for everything captured
- An **Archive** state, usually a checkbox or a status
- **Relation properties** tying a note to its project and a project to its area

That last line matters more than it looks. PARA decides **where a note goes**. Relations decide **what it is connected to**. The first is a folder question. The second is the reason to keep notes at all.

## Where it stalls

For the first month every note is recent and you remember it. Around the few-hundred mark, three symptoms appear.

- **A note you wrote and cannot find.** You remember the idea, not the title or the project it went under. Search needs a word you no longer recall
- **The same topic started twice.** Two notes on one subject, in two different areas, each unaware of the other
- **Material linked once and forgotten.** A resource was related to a project that is now archived, and nothing active points at it any more

All three are the same problem seen from different sides: the connections exist, but there is no way to look at them.

## Why better filing does not fix it

The instinct is to reorganise: more tags, a stricter PARA, a new dashboard. That improves the answer to "where does this go?" and does nothing for "what is this connected to?".

Notion already records the connections as relation properties, subpages and database membership. The record is fine. What is missing is **a screen that lays it all out at once**. Tables and boards show one database at a time, as rows. A relation column shows the ties of one row. Nothing shows the whole.

## What helps inside Notion

Some habits reduce the damage without any extra tool.

- **Relate every note to at least one thing when you capture it.** A note with an empty relation column is the one you will lose
- **Add a filtered view for unlinked notes.** In the Notes database, filter for "Project is empty" and review that view weekly
- **Use rollups on projects** to count related notes. A project with zero is either new or dead
- **Mention instead of retyping.** `@` a page and you get a backlink for free

These work, but each is one database at a time. The view of unlinked notes in Notes says nothing about orphaned pages in Resources.

## Seeing the whole thing

Notion-mind reads the connections your second brain already has and draws them as one graph. Notes and databases are nodes, membership and subpages are solid lines, relations are dotted.

![A Notion workspace drawn as a graph: database nodes, page nodes and relation lines](/shots/graph-overview.png)

The three symptoms map onto three things you can do on that screen.

- **Cannot find a note:** open the project or area you half remember and look at what hangs off it. Hovering a node keeps only its neighbourhood sharp
- **Duplicate topics:** two clusters that should be one are visible as two clusters
- **Forgotten material:** one filter leaves only the pages no relation touches, across every database at once

It is rebuilt from the workspace each time you open it, so it adds nothing to maintain. For the note-linking side of the same idea, see [Building a knowledge graph in Notion](/blog/notion-knowledge-graph).

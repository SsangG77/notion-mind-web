# Does Notion have a graph view?

No. Notion has no graph view like Obsidian's. A database can be shown as a table, board, timeline, calendar, list, gallery or chart, and every one of those shows a single database as rows. Nothing in Notion lays out how your pages connect to each other on one screen.

## What a graph view is

A graph view draws each note as a dot (a node) and each link between two notes as a line. It answers questions a list cannot: which notes are hubs, which sit alone, which two topics are joined by a single page. Obsidian made the idea popular, and it is the feature people coming from Obsidian ask about first.

## What Notion gives you instead

Notion has four ways to see a connection, and each one shows a slice.

- **The sidebar and breadcrumbs** show which page sits inside which. That is the page tree, and only the tree
- **Backlinks** list the pages that mention or link to the page you are on. One page at a time, and they leave out relations and membership. [Notion backlinks](/blog/notion-backlinks) covers where they appear and why they sometimes do not
- **Relation properties** show, in one column, which rows in another database a row is tied to. [Relation vs rollup](/blog/notion-relation-property) explains how they work
- **Database views** show the rows of one database as a table, board or timeline

All four are accurate. None of them is a picture. To know how a project page, the meeting notes that mention it, and the tasks related to it hang together, you open each one and hold the result in your head.

## Why people want it anyway

A workspace of fifty pages does not need a graph. A workspace of a thousand does, for three reasons.

1. **Finding what you forgot.** A page that nothing links to does not show up in any list unless you already know its name
2. **Seeing clusters.** Whether your notes on a subject form one group or three unconnected ones is invisible in a table
3. **Tracing a path.** "How did this note end up related to that project?" is a question about several hops, and backlinks answer one hop at a time

## The workarounds, and where each one stops

**Drawing it by hand.** A whiteboard tool embedded in a Notion page gives you a picture, but the picture is separate from the links in Notion. Add a page and the drawing is already out of date.

**Exporting to another app.** Notion can export pages as Markdown, and Obsidian can open the result. Page links survive in part, relation properties and database structure do not come across intact, and the export is a snapshot that starts ageing at once.

**Connecting a tool that reads the workspace.** Notion's API exposes pages, databases and relation properties, so an outside tool can read the real connections and draw them. The picture then matches the workspace, because it is built from the workspace.

## What Notion-mind draws

Notion-mind is the third kind. It reads three things your workspace already records:

- which database each page belongs to
- which page sits inside which page
- where each relation property points

Each becomes a line, and every page and database becomes a node. Membership and subpages are solid lines; relations are dotted, so you can tell structure from cross-links at a glance. The map is rebuilt from the workspace every time you open it, so there is nothing to maintain.

![A Notion workspace drawn as a graph: database nodes, page nodes and relation lines](/shots/graph-overview.png)

Hover a node and everything it is not connected to dims, which is the closest thing to Obsidian's local graph. Click it for its properties, its relations and a link straight into Notion. One filter leaves only the pages no relation touches.

There is an example graph on the home page that needs no sign-in, if you want to see how it behaves before connecting anything. For how the two apps compare beyond this one screen, see [Notion vs Obsidian](/blog/notion-vs-obsidian-graph-view).
